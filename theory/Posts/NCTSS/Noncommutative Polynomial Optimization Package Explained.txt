Report on NCTSSoS.jl: A Comprehensive Framework for Sparse Non-Commutative Polynomial Optimization
1. Executive Summary
The optimization of polynomials over non-commuting variables constitutes a foundational challenge in applied mathematics, theoretical physics, and control theory. Unlike classical optimization, where variables commute (i.e.,   ), non-commutative (NC) optimization deals with operators on Hilbert spaces, elements of C*-algebras, and matrices of arbitrary dimension. This mathematical setting is indigenous to quantum mechanics, where physical observables are represented by Hermitian operators that do not necessarily commute. The package NCTSSoS.jl, developed within the high-performance Julia programming ecosystem, represents a paradigm shift in solving these problems. It is designed to overcome the prohibitive computational costs associated with standard Semidefinite Programming (SDP) relaxations by exploiting a specific structural property known as term sparsity.
Standard approaches, such as the Lasserre or Navascués-Pironio-Acín (NPA) hierarchies, construct a sequence of SDP relaxations that converge to the global optimum. However, these methods suffer from the "curse of dimensionality," where the size of the constraint matrices grows exponentially with the number of variables and the relaxation order. Consequently, dense hierarchies are often limited to toy problems involving fewer than a dozen variables. NCTSSoS.jl (Non-Commutative Term Sparsity Sum-of-Squares) addresses this bottleneck by analyzing the constituent monomials of the input polynomials to construct a Term Sparsity Pattern (TSP) graph. By performing a chordal extension and clique decomposition on this graph, the software decomposes large, intractable SDP constraints into smaller, manageable blocks.
This report provides an exhaustive analysis of NCTSSoS.jl. It details the package's theoretical underpinnings, contrasting term sparsity with correlative sparsity; explores its algorithmic architecture and integration with the Julia scientific stack; and rigorously examines its applications in Quantum Information Science (QIS), specifically in bounding ground state energies of many-body Hamiltonians and certifying violations of Bell inequalities. Furthermore, the report benchmarks the tool against legacy software such as Ncpol2sdpa, demonstrating its capability to handle systems with thousands of variables—a scale previously unreachable for certified global optimization in the non-commutative setting.

3. The NCTSSoS Algorithm: Exploiting Sparsity
The defining innovation of NCTSSoS.jl is its departure from the dense relaxation paradigm. Instead of assuming that every monomial interacts with every other monomial, the package analyzes the input data to construct a sparse structural representation of the problem. This relies on distinguishing between two types of sparsity: Correlative Sparsity and Term Sparsity.
3.1 Correlative Sparsity vs. Term Sparsity
A nuanced understanding of the difference between these two forms of sparsity is critical for utilizing the package effectively.
3.1.1 Correlative Sparsity (CS)
Correlative sparsity, extensively studied by researchers like Klep and Povh, focuses on the interaction between variables. If the objective function    is a sum of terms where each term only involves a small subset of variables (e.g.,   ), the variables can be partitioned into cliques.
   * Mechanism: The large moment matrix is replaced by several smaller matrices, each corresponding to a clique of variables.
   * Limitation: If the polynomial contains a single term that couples all variables (e.g.,   ), or if the interaction graph is dense, correlative sparsity offers no advantage. The cliques merge into one giant block, and the method reverts to the dense case.7
3.1.2 Term Sparsity (TS)
Term sparsity, the namesake of the package, operates at a much finer granularity. It looks at the interactions between monomials (terms), rather than variables. Even if all variables are coupled, the set of monomials appearing in the SOHS decomposition might be sparse.
      * Mechanism: NCTSSoS constructs a graph where nodes are basis monomials. An edge represents a non-zero entry in the Gram matrix of the SOHS decomposition.
      * Advantage: This method can reduce the problem size even when correlative sparsity fails (e.g., in systems with global coupling but sparse coefficients).
      * Combined Approach: The package offers a unique feature, CS-TSSOS, which exploits both forms of sparsity simultaneously. It first partitions variables into cliques (CS) and then applies term sparsity (TS) within each clique, enabling scaling to thousands of variables.1

3.3 Chordal Extension and Matrix Decomposition
A sparse graph alone is insufficient for efficient optimization; the sparsity must be structured. Specifically, the graph must be chordal.
               * Chordality: A graph is chordal if every cycle of length strictly greater than three possesses a chord (an edge connecting two non-consecutive vertices of the cycle). 4. Software Architecture and Implementation
NCTSSoS.jl leverages the Julia programming language's unique strengths—specifically its type system, multiple dispatch, and just-in-time (JIT) compilation—to build a flexible and high-performance solver stack.
4.1 Integration with the Julia Ecosystem
The package does not operate in isolation but is a component of a modular ecosystem for polynomial optimization.
                        * DynamicPolynomials.jl & MultivariatePolynomials.jl: These libraries provide the symbolic backbone. NCTSSoS uses them to define non-commutative variables. The macro @ncpolyvar creates symbolic objects that respect non-commutative multiplication rules (  ). This seamless integration allows users to write natural algebraic expressions (e.g., f = x^2 + y*x + 1) rather than defining coefficient matrices manually.1
                        * JuMP.jl (Julia for Mathematical Programming):
Perhaps the most significant architectural choice is the use of JuMP as the modeling layer. JuMP is a domain-specific language for mathematical optimization. By building the SDP relaxation within JuMP, NCTSSoS becomes solver-agnostic. It allows the user to switch between backend solvers without changing a single line of problem definition code.
                        * ChordalGraph.jl: This auxiliary package, maintained by the same authors, handles the graph-theoretic heavy lifting. It implements the algorithms for detecting chordality, computing chordal extensions (using heuristics like Minimum Degree or Maximal Cardinality Search), and finding maximal cliques. Separating this logic ensures that improvements in graph algorithms automatically benefit the optimization routines.10

5. Applications in Quantum Information Science
The primary impetus for the development of NCTSSoS.jl stems from the rigorous demands of Quantum Information Science (QIS). In this field, researchers often need to bound quantities (like energy or correlation strength) over all possible Hilbert spaces, a task perfectly suited for the dimension-free nature of the Moment-SOHS hierarchy.

A flagship application of NCTSSoS is the analysis of Bell inequalities. These mathematical inequalities distinguish between correlations explainable by classical local hidden variable theories and those requiring quantum entanglement.

6. Performance and Benchmarking
The practical utility of NCTSSoS.jl is best understood through its performance relative to alternative tools.
6.1 Comparison with Ncpol2sdpa
For many years, Ncpol2sdpa (written in Python by Peter Wittek) was the standard tool for generating non-commutative SDP relaxations.
                                    * Algorithm: Ncpol2sdpa typically generates the full moment matrix for a given relaxation order. While it supports some sparsity features, it lacks the sophisticated term-wise chordal extension of NCTSSoS.
                                    * Benchmarks: In side-by-side comparisons reported in the literature, NCTSSoS consistently outperforms Ncpol2sdpa on sparse problems. For a randomly generated sparse non-commutative polynomial with 100 variables, Ncpol2sdpa may fail to generate the SDP due to memory exhaustion (OOM), whereas NCTSSoS can generate and solve the decomposed SDP in seconds or minutes.19
                                    * Maintenance: Ncpol2sdpa is no longer actively maintained. NCTSSoS benefits from the active development of the Julia optimization stack (updates to JuMP, newer solvers).
6.2 Scalability Analysis
The scalability of NCTSSoS is not uniform; it depends heavily on the sparsity topology of the input problem.
                                    * Chain/Ring Topology: (Common in 1D physics). The TSP graph has small treewidth. NCTSSoS scales linearly with the number of variables   . Problems with    are feasible.
                                    * Star Topology: (One central variable interacts with everyone). The central variable couples all cliques. Performance degrades but is still better than dense methods.
                                    * Complete Graph: (All-to-all interaction). Sparsity exploitation is impossible. NCTSSoS reverts to the performance of a dense solver, limited to   .5
The inclusion of the CS-TSSOS (Combined Sparsity) module allows the solver to handle "blocks of blocks," pushing the scalability boundary further for systems that have a hierarchical modular structure.6

7. User Guide: Practical Workflows and Syntax
This section provides a practical guide for users looking to implement optimization routines using NCTSSoS.jl.
7.1 Defining the Problem
The workflow begins with importing the necessary modules and defining the algebra.


Julia




using NCTSSoS
using DynamicPolynomials
using MosekTools # or COSMO

# Define non-commuting variables
@ncpolyvar x[1:4]

7.2 Constructing the Objective and Constraints
Users must be careful to respect the non-commutative nature of the variables during definition.


Julia




# Objective: A simple polynomial
# Note: x*x is distinct from x*x
f = 1 + x^4 + x^4 + x*x + x*x

# Constraints: Example of a sphere constraint and a commutator
constraints = [
   1 - x^2 - x^2,    # Inequality: 1 - x1^2 - x2^2 >= 0
   x*x - x*x   # Equality: [x1, x2] = 0 (handled via numeq)
]

7.3 Executing the Hierarchy
The primary interface is the nctssos_first function, which initializes the hierarchy.


Julia




# Run the first step of the hierarchy (relaxation order d)
# TS="block" enables the maximal chordal extension strategy
opt, data = nctssos_first(
   [f; constraints], 
   x, 
   d=2, 
   numeq=1,      # The last 1 constraint is an equality
   obj="eigen",  # Minimizing the minimum eigenvalue
   TS="block",
   solver=Mosek.Optimizer
)

Key Parameters:
                                       * numeq: Specifies how many of the provided constraints are equalities (  ) rather than inequalities (  ). This is crucial for performance, as equalities are handled by ideal reduction or kernel methods which are cheaper than PSD localizing matrices.
                                       * TS: The Term Sparsity strategy. "block" is generally robust. "MD" (Minimum Degree) can sometimes find better clique decompositions for irregular graphs.
                                       * obj: "eigen" for ground states, "trace" for tracial states.
7.4 Iterative Refinement
If the bound obtained at order    is not sufficient (checked via the duality gap or physical intuition), the user can refine the hierarchy without restarting from scratch:


Julia




# Compute higher steps of the hierarchy
opt, data = nctssos_higher!(data)

This function utilizes the data structures computed in the first step to incrementally add higher-order moments, optimizing memory usage.

8. Conclusion
NCTSSoS.jl stands as a critical infrastructure tool in the modern landscape of computational mathematics and quantum physics. By bridging the gap between abstract operator theory and high-performance numerical optimization, it enables the certification of properties for quantum systems that were previously inaccessible.
Its theoretical contribution lies in the rigorous application of term sparsity and chordal decomposition to the non-commutative Moment-SOHS hierarchy. This innovation breaks the "curse of dimensionality" for a broad class of structured problems, allowing for the transition from analyzing 10-qubit toys to modeling 1000-qubit chains.
For the practitioner in Quantum Information Science, NCTSSoS.jl offers a robust, open-source alternative to legacy codes, backed by the speed and flexibility of the Julia ecosystem. Whether for bounding the ground state energy of a new material or testing the limits of non-locality in a quantum network, NCTSSoS.jl provides the necessary mathematical rigor and computational efficiency to derive certified, provable bounds. As the field of quantum computing matures, the need for such classical verification tools will only grow, cementing NCTSSoS.jl's role as a foundational utility in the quantum software stack.
